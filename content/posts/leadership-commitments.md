I have people reporting to me in my new role. I recognize this affects not only my own work and career, but theirs as well. So as I've been spending time thinking about leadership, I've decided my image of it is best phrased as commitments. To those who report to me:

*   I will view you as a person, not a role.
*   I will remember that you have a life outside of work, and that in most cases it comes first.
*   I will respect that you may or may not share my beliefs, interests, background, faith, identity, hobbies, race, gender, physical abilities, sexual orientation, or personality.
*   To the best of my ability, I will not let those differences affect our work relationship.
*   I will not play favorites with those who are more like me.
*   I will commit to a continuous process of recognizing and addressing my own biases.

*   I will value people over process.
*   I will try to improve our practices to make your job easier and our work better.
*   But I will give more priority to you than to those practices.

*   I will put your career interests ahead of my own.
*   I will actively look for ways to honor your accomplishments and growth.
*   I will encourage you to continue growing in your professional skills and abilities.
*   I will speak up for you, shamelessly.

*   If there is something that needs to be addressed with your work, I will be up front and honest about it rather than letting it fester.
*   I will humbly recall my own faults when considering yours, knowing that we are all human and make mistakes.

*   If you bring an idea, concern, or problem to me, I will listen fully to what you have to say before making a judgment.
*   I will respect that you have unique ideas and perspectives that have value even if I disagree with them.

*   If I have good news to share with you, I will do so happily, but I will not pretend that it is all my doing or that you owe me for it.
*   If I have bad news to share with you, I will do so as directly and promptly as possible.

*   I will admit that I may fall short of these commitments or your expectations of me, and that you have a right to speak up when that happens.
*   I will not hold it against you when you do.

I expect this list to grow and change. I still have a lot to learn, as we all do. But I hope that the process of forming these commitments, thinking about them, and even revisiting them will help me become a better leader.
