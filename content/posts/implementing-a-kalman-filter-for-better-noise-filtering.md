Yesterday I shared the first of a 2-part series tackling noisy sensor readings. It set up the case study--monitoring the water depth and change of a municipal stormwater system--and used basic software techniques to look past the variations inherent to real-world sensor data.  
  
In this 2nd part, we are approach the same problem using a Kalman Filter, a widely used approach to dealing with uncertainty in a probabilistically sound way. After setting up the filter and looking at the results, we compare its performance to the simpler techniques from part 1. We find they're both great at reducing noise, but one forms markedly better.
