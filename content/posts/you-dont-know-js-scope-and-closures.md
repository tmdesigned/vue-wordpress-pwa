Kyle Simpson's _You Don't Know JS_ series attacks JavaScript from a brilliant angle. He doesn't just explain code fragments or directly teach techniques. Rather, he explains _how_ JavaScript is working under the surface so that the user knows _why_ its working the way it is. This depth is not necessary for the average practitioner who "just needs it to work," but for those who want to take their skills to the next level it is exactly what is needed. The 6-book series, originally (and still) published through GitHub, is available in ~100-200 page volumes that tackle a particular issue. The first is to ensure readers are up to par with the basics, while this book, the second in the series, jumps into scope and closures. I appreciate that the author deftly toes the line between depth and accessibility. For instance, in detailing the subtleties of the JavaScript parsing engine, Simpson presents the process as a friendly dialogue between its different components. More than just providing humor, this type of thoughtful approach actually makes the concepts clearer. The order of the material makes perfect sense. He begins with how the JavaScript parsing process (i.e. "lexing") works, expands to its implications on scope, details the techniques used to "tame" it, and finally builds to the abstract concept of closures. Here the rest of the book comes together into what Simpson describes as a Matrix-like awakening: that you've been using closures all along, just without seeing them. Simply put, this is a fantastic book that sits just above the knowledge plateau where I've seen so many web developers get stuck. They won't come out on the other side of this read with an abundance of new skills, although there are a few. But they **will** understand what they've already been doing (or struggling to do) better.
